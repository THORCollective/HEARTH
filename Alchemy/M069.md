  A device enrolled as a residential proxy exit node stops behaving like a leaf. It carries traffic it did not originate, for destinations unrelated to its function, on behalf of strangers - so its flow record develops properties a legitimate embedded device never has: near-symmetric byte ratios, a destination set that grows without bound, session concurrency far above its own workload, round-the-clock activity with no diurnal shape, and one or two long-lived control sessions alongside the churn. No single threshold generalises across device classes or sites, so a model that scores each device against the distribution of its own model cohort will surface the enrolled units while leaving the merely talkative alone.

false_positive_notes: |
  The devices this model scores are legitimately noisy, which is why cohort normalisation rather than absolute thresholds is the whole design.
  - Streaming and content-delivery devices move large volumes and talk to many addresses. A media player pulling video is high-volume and strongly asymmetric, and the asymmetry is what separates it from an exit node - so volume alone must never contribute positively to the score without the ratio feature.
  - Peer-assisted content delivery exists legitimately in some consumer platforms and produces genuine upstream traffic to peers. If a device class is known to do this by design, it belongs in its own cohort rather than in the general one, and the finding becomes the presence of that device class on a corporate network at all.
  - Conferencing and telehealth endpoints produce symmetric flows by nature, since audio and video run both ways. Cohort them separately and weight on destination-set growth and off-hours activity instead of on symmetry.
  - Software update windows produce fleet-wide bursts that look like coordinated activity. Exclude known update endpoints and vendor CDNs before scoring, and treat a synchronous fleet-wide spike as a deployment signature rather than a detection.
  - Guest and BYOD segments contain real user devices whose behaviour legitimately resembles this. Restrict the model to segments whose inventory says they hold only fixed-function devices, otherwise the top of the ranking fills with phones.
  - A high score is a lead, not a verdict. Confirmation comes from pairing it with the control-channel evidence in [[H461]] or from a packet capture showing traffic for destinations the device has no reason to reach.
notes: |
  Source reporting is Google Threat Intelligence Group's November 2, 2026 account of the XCPE botnet takedown, which documents the monetization layer in unusual detail. The control-channel half of the same activity is hunted in [[H461]]; the nearest existing hunt is [[H226]], which covers a proxyware ELF dropped on a Linux host after code execution - this model differs in that there is no code-execution step to find, no host telemetry at all, and the unit of analysis is a flow record rather than a process tree.
  Why a model rather than a rule - Google reports that a single device may run several modules providing the same or similar functionality, with as many as three residential proxy SDKs installed on one device, because the operator sought to monetize every infected device to the greatest extent possible. The named modules span several commercial residential-proxy and bandwidth-sharing brands, and Google documents a resource-constraint configuration pushed over the control channel that caps CPU at a set percentage, caps read and write rates, and assigns each module a relative priority weight for system resources. That constraint mechanism is the crux of the modelling problem: the operator deliberately throttles the device to stay inside its normal envelope, so any fixed threshold is a number the adversary is already tuning against, while the shape of the traffic cannot be hidden without ceasing to be a proxy.
  Feature engineering, per device per day over the modelling window:
  - Byte symmetry - the ratio of upstream to downstream bytes, aggregated per day and also as a per-session distribution. Legitimate media devices are strongly downstream-dominant; an exit node relays, so its per-session ratios cluster near parity and its daily ratio rises steadily.
  - Destination cardinality and growth - distinct remote addresses and distinct remote autonomous systems per day, plus the slope of the cumulative distinct-destination curve. A fixed-function device reaches a small, stable set that plateaus. An exit node's set never plateaus, because its destinations are chosen by whoever rented it.
  - Destination novelty - the fraction of a day's destinations never seen from that cohort before. This separates a device following its vendor's CDN from one following strangers.
  - Session concurrency - the maximum number of simultaneously open sessions, and the count of sessions shorter than five seconds. Relaying produces high concurrency and heavy short-session churn; both the reported Java-based multiplexed tunnel and the described per-client queue architecture imply many sessions managed at once.
  - Diurnal flatness - the variance of hourly byte volume across the day, and the ratio of out-of-hours to in-hours traffic. A signage panel in a closed store and a media box in an empty room should go quiet. An exit node is most valuable when residential-looking addresses are expected to be active, so its activity flattens.
  - Protocol and transport mix - the share of traffic on QUIC and raw UDP versus TCP, and the presence of long-lived WebSocket sessions. Google documents gateway families reached over UDP and QUIC, and several modules that maintain a single long-lived WebSocket to multiplex control messages and tunnelled connections over TLS, including one that also supports bulk content tunnelling.
  - Control-channel persistence - the count and duration of sessions lasting longer than twelve hours with low average throughput and regular keep-alives. This is the feature that ties the model back to [[H461]], and it is what distinguishes an enrolled device from one having an unusual day.
  - Heartbeat regularity - autocorrelation of inter-arrival times to the longest-lived destinations, since the described registration-and-heartbeat pattern produces periodicity that ordinary application traffic does not.
  Model approach - group devices into cohorts by vendor and model from the asset inventory, which is the only grouping that makes the comparison meaningful. Within each cohort, standardise each feature and score with an unsupervised outlier method (isolation forest or a robust Mahalanobis distance over the feature vector); a cohort too small for density estimation falls back to a ranked comparison on the two most discriminating features, symmetry and destination growth. Fit on a sixty-day window and score daily, keeping a rolling ranking rather than a binary verdict, because the useful output is "the three most proxy-shaped devices in this cohort this week" and not an alert count. Fire conditions worth escalating without waiting for the ranking: a device whose daily upstream-to-downstream ratio crosses parity for three consecutive days; a device whose distinct-destination count exceeds its own cohort median by an order of magnitude; or any device in a fixed-function cohort holding a session longer than twenty-four hours to a destination outside its vendor's infrastructure.
  Tuning and allowlists - exclude vendor update and CDN endpoints, internal management addresses, and NTP and DNS before feature extraction, since they inflate destination counts without carrying signal. Maintain the cohort table from inventory and refresh it when new device models are deployed, because an unrecognised model scored against the wrong cohort is the most common source of a wrong ranking. Carry a per-site egress normaliser so a device at a large site is not penalised for sharing a busy circuit.
  Validation without a known-bad sample - the model can be checked against the installed base rather than against malware. Deploy a consenting test device running a commercial bandwidth-sharing client on an isolated segment, generate the same feature vector, and confirm it ranks at the top of its cohort. That is a legitimate, reproducible positive control, and it also quantifies how much the resource-constraint mechanism suppresses each feature when the client is throttled.
  What to do with a confirmed hit - the device cannot be remediated, since the backdoor persists in the firmware partition and reinstalls after a reboot, so the response is removal or network isolation of the model line across all sites. Pivot to the purchasing record: Google's finding is that the malicious code was built into many device models and brands over multiple years, which means a confirmed unit implicates the supplier and the product family rather than the individual asset.

# M069

The XCPE takedown report is unusually frank about where the money came from. The manufacturer sold backdoor access to affiliates, and those affiliates' modules turned devices into residential proxy exit nodes, ad-fraud engines, or loaders for still more modules. Google names the proxy and bandwidth-sharing brands involved, and notes that a single device sometimes ran three residential proxy SDKs at once - the operator monetizing the same unit several ways in parallel.

For a defender, the control channel is the cleaner detection and the monetization is the more durable one. Control infrastructure gets taken down, rotated, and rebuilt; the commercial proxy brands keep operating, because enrolling devices is their business model and the fraud is in how consent was obtained. A device that stops beaconing to a seized broker may still be selling your bandwidth through an SDK that was configured months ago.

The problem is that the devices this applies to are legitimately strange. A media player moves enormous volume. A signage panel talks to a CDN all day. A telehealth endpoint runs symmetric audio and video. There is no threshold that holds across those classes, and the operator is actively tuning against thresholds anyway - the configuration pushed to infected devices caps CPU and I/O per module precisely so the device stays inside its normal envelope.

What cannot be tuned away is the shape. A proxy carries traffic it did not ask for, to destinations it has no relationship with, for people who are awake when the building is empty. That is a vector of features, compared against devices of the same make and model, which is what this hunt scores.

## Why

- Thresholds are the adversary's parameter and shape is not. The reported configuration explicitly throttles each module's CPU and I/O to keep the device unremarkable, which defeats volume-based rules by design. Symmetry, unbounded destination growth and diurnal flatness survive throttling, because removing them would stop the device being a usable exit node.
- Cohort comparison is what makes the problem tractable. Absolute numbers are meaningless across media players, signage and clinical kiosks, but within one make and model the distribution is tight enough that an enrolled unit stands out - and the asset inventory that supplies the cohort grouping is something the environment should have anyway.
- The monetization layer outlives the control layer. Takedowns remove brokers and C2 domains; they do not remove commercial proxy SDKs already configured on devices, and Google notes the affiliates have diversified distribution well beyond this one backdoor. A model keyed on proxy behaviour keeps working after the infrastructure half of the hunt goes quiet.
- These devices offer no host telemetry and no remediation path. The backdoor is a firmware preload that reinstalls itself after a reboot, so there is nothing to scan and nothing to clean. Flow-shape scoring is the only instrument available, and it runs on data most networks already retain.
- The model has an honest positive control. A consenting test device running a commercial bandwidth-sharing client reproduces the target behaviour on demand, which means the scoring can be validated and calibrated without waiting for an incident - including measuring how much the throttling mechanism suppresses each feature.
