title: "Scoring Slack API callers on inter-arrival distribution — a bounded-uniform 150-to-450-second poll from a non-interactive Linux process is a beacon, not a chat client"
hypothesis: >-
  A backdoor that uses a corporate SaaS platform for command and control cannot be found by
  reputation, because the destination is a service the organisation pays for and allows. Mandiant
  observed ARMORY CASTLE deploy a Go backdoor it tracks as LAZYBONES to a Linux appliance as
  /usr/bin/vmtoolscs, which establishes C2 over HTTPS using Slack, periodically checking the most
  recent message in a designated Slack channel for commands, encrypting those messages with
  AES-256-CTR before Base64 encoding them, and polling with a 150 to 450 second jitter interval. It
  supports four commands - cmd, upload, download and sleep - and the actor persisted it with a
  systemd service file set to restart every 50 minutes. A rule-based detection cannot separate that
  from legitimate Slack traffic without an allowlist of every process in the estate that may talk to
  Slack, which no one maintains. A model can, because the timing distribution is the giveaway. A
  randomised sleep drawn from a bounded uniform range has a signature no human-driven client
  produces - a mean of 300 seconds, a standard deviation of about 86.6 seconds, a coefficient of
  variation near 0.29, a minimum that never falls below 150 seconds and a maximum that never exceeds
  450, roughly 288 requests a day, and crucially no diurnal shape at all. Real Slack clients cluster
  around working hours, go quiet overnight, and have heavy-tailed gaps measured in minutes to hours.
  Score every internal source that talks to the Slack API on inter-arrival regularity, bounded
  support, diurnal flatness, request and response size uniformity, and session-count periodicity,
  and rank the outliers. The highest-scoring sources will be automation, and one of them may not be
  yours.

false_positive_notes: |
  The top of this ranking will be full of legitimate automation, and that is expected rather than a flaw. CI runners, alerting integrations, monitoring bots, deployment pipelines and chat-ops tooling all poll Slack on fixed or jittered schedules and will score highly on regularity and diurnal flatness. The model's job is to produce a short ranked list, and the analyst's job is to resolve each entry to a process and reconcile it against the approved-automation register. Expect the register to be incomplete the first time it is used; completing it is part of the first run's output.

  Fixed-interval automation is actually easier to separate than it looks. A cron-driven poller has a coefficient of variation near zero and interval values clustered on a single number; a bounded-uniform jitter has a coefficient of variation near 0.29 and interval values spread evenly across a 300-second band with hard edges. Score both and keep them as separate classes rather than merging them into one anomaly bucket.

  Mobile clients and laptops roaming between networks produce ragged timing that can superficially resemble jitter. They differ in diurnal shape, in source-address stability and in response-size distribution, because a real client fetches conversation content of widely varying size while a poller reads one message.

  The specific numbers in this hunt are derived, not observed. Mandiant states the polling interval as 150 to 450 seconds and the restart period as 50 minutes; the mean of 300 seconds, the standard deviation of about 86.6 seconds, the coefficient of variation near 0.29, the figure of roughly 288 requests a day and the expectation of about 10 polls per restart window are all computed from those two statements on the assumption that the draw is uniform across the stated range. Mandiant does not say the distribution is uniform. Treat the band edges as the reliable part and the distribution shape as a modelling assumption to validate against whatever the environment actually shows.

  The systemd restart period is a strong secondary feature but it is also the easiest parameter for an operator to change, and Mandiant notes LAZYBONES supports a `sleep` command specifically for adjusting the beacon interval. A compromised host under active operation may show the timing signature change mid-capture, which will look like the anomaly disappearing. Score over multiple windows and keep the history.

  Encryption means payload inspection adds nothing here. The content is AES-256-CTR encrypted before Base64 encoding, so entropy of the decoded body is uniformly high and is not a discriminator against other encrypted traffic. Size uniformity, not content, is the usable payload feature.
notes: |
  Feature set to compute, per source address and per destination service, over a rolling 14-day window with a minimum of 100 observations before a source is scored.

  - `interval_mean`, `interval_median`, `interval_sd`, `interval_cv` - the inter-arrival statistics. For LAZYBONES as reported the expected values are a mean near 300 seconds and a coefficient of variation near 0.29.
  - `interval_min`, `interval_max`, `interval_p01`, `interval_p99` - the support bounds. A hard floor and ceiling with nothing outside them is the bounded-uniform fingerprint; human traffic has no floor and a long tail.
  - `uniformity_score` - a goodness-of-fit statistic for the uniform distribution over the observed support, for example a Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic against `Uniform(min, max)`. High fit plus a wide support band separates jittered beacons from fixed-interval cron.
  - `diurnal_flatness` - the ratio of request volume in the organisation's working hours to volume outside them, and the entropy of the 24-bin hourly histogram. A poller is flat; a client is not.
  - `weekend_ratio` - weekend volume over weekday volume. Automation is near 1.0, human clients are far below it.
  - `req_size_cv` and `resp_size_cv` - coefficients of variation for bytes sent and received. Reading one message repeatedly produces tight size distributions; real client traffic does not.
  - `distinct_uri_paths` and `path_entropy` - a backdoor polls a small fixed set of API methods; a client touches many.
  - `session_restart_periodicity` - the dominant period in a Fourier or autocorrelation analysis of session-start times, which is where a systemd unit restarting on a fixed schedule shows up as a secondary peak independent of the poll jitter.
  - `process_interactivity` - whether the initiating process has a TTY, a user session, or a parent in a desktop or shell lineage. A poller run by a systemd unit on an appliance has none of these.

  Scoring and ranking. Fit an unsupervised model on the feature matrix - isolation forest or a simple distance-to-centroid on standardised features both work - and rank sources by anomaly score. Then apply two deterministic overrides that promote a source regardless of its model score, because each is individually damning: `interval_cv` between 0.22 and 0.36 with `interval_min` at or above 120 seconds and `interval_max` at or below 480 seconds, which brackets the reported band with tolerance; and `diurnal_flatness` within 20 percent of 1.0 combined with `process_interactivity` false on a host that is an appliance or server rather than a workstation.

  A starting implementation over proxy logs, computing the features in SQL before the model step. Replace the service-hostname predicate with whatever the local proxy records for the Slack API.

      WITH events AS (
        SELECT src_ip,
               ts,
               bytes_sent,
               bytes_received,
               uri_path,
               LAG(ts) OVER (PARTITION BY src_ip ORDER BY ts) AS prev_ts
        FROM proxy_log
        WHERE ts >= NOW() - INTERVAL '14 days'
          AND dest_host LIKE '%.slack.com'
      ),
      gaps AS (
        SELECT src_ip,
               EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (ts - prev_ts)) AS gap_s,
               bytes_sent,
               bytes_received,
               uri_path,
               EXTRACT(HOUR FROM ts) AS hr,
               EXTRACT(ISODOW FROM ts) AS dow
        FROM events
        WHERE prev_ts IS NOT NULL
      )
      SELECT src_ip,
             COUNT(*) AS n,
             AVG(gap_s) AS interval_mean,
             STDDEV_SAMP(gap_s) AS interval_sd,
             STDDEV_SAMP(gap_s) / NULLIF(AVG(gap_s), 0) AS interval_cv,
             MIN(gap_s) AS interval_min,
             MAX(gap_s) AS interval_max,
             STDDEV_SAMP(bytes_received) / NULLIF(AVG(bytes_received), 0) AS resp_size_cv,
             COUNT(DISTINCT uri_path) AS distinct_paths,
             SUM(CASE WHEN hr BETWEEN 8 AND 18 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)::float
               / NULLIF(SUM(CASE WHEN hr NOT BETWEEN 8 AND 18 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), 0) AS work_hour_ratio,
             SUM(CASE WHEN dow IN (6, 7) THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)::float / COUNT(*) AS weekend_share
      FROM gaps
      GROUP BY src_ip
      HAVING COUNT(*) >= 100
      ORDER BY interval_cv ASC, distinct_paths ASC;

  The ordering deliberately puts the most regular, narrowest-API-surface callers first. Read down that list and resolve each source to a process before scoring anything as anomalous; the ranking is a work queue, not a verdict.

  Resolving a hit to a process and a persistence mechanism. For each flagged source, pull the initiating process from endpoint telemetry and then read its systemd unit. Mandiant records LAZYBONES persisted through a systemd service file set to restart every 50 minutes, and staged as `/usr/bin/vmtoolscs`, which is a name chosen to read as a VMware Tools component on a virtual appliance. Two checks on the host:

      systemctl list-units --type=service --all --no-pager | grep -iE 'vmtool|vmware'
      systemd-analyze verify /etc/systemd/system/*.service 2>&1 | head -40

  Then compare each unit's `ExecStart` path against package ownership - a unit pointing at a binary in `/usr/bin` that no package owns is the finding. Strong red flags: a `RestartSec` or timer period near 3000 seconds; an `ExecStart` binary with no package owner; a VMware-sounding service name on a host that is not running VMware Tools; the binary is a Go ELF with no vendor signature.

  Workspace-side corroboration. Because the C2 channel is the organisation's own Slack workspace, the workspace audit log is a first-party source for this hunt. Look for an app, bot token or user token making `conversations.history` or equivalent read calls against one channel at a fixed cadence, and for channels created outside normal governance that have a single automation reader and no human members. Reconcile every app and bot against the approved-apps inventory. An unapproved token polling one channel every few minutes is the same beacon seen from the other end, and it is visible without any network telemetry at all.

  Scope note: HEARTH covers SaaS and web-service C2 from other angles, and this hunt is deliberately the timing-distribution one. M019 scores outbound TLS sessions on JA3 rarity, beacon periodicity, certificate novelty and cookie-header entropy for a specific implant family, which is a session-fingerprint model rather than an inter-arrival-distribution model, and it does not address a destination the organisation legitimately allows. The Flames hunts in this batch cover the rest of the same intrusion - H493 the vCenter exploitation, H494 the signing-key theft, H495 the appliance credential theft, H496 the SSH backdoor, H497 the hypervisor-side disk extraction - and LAZYBONES is the channel that outlives all of them, which is why it is worth modelling rather than signaturing.

# M073

Mandiant and Google Threat Intelligence Group's November 19, 2026 analysis of ARMORY CASTLE records a backdoor it tracks as LAZYBONES deployed to a Linux appliance as `/usr/bin/vmtoolscs`, alongside an SSH private key, and persisted with a systemd service file set to restart every 50 minutes. It is a lightweight Go backdoor that establishes command and control over HTTPS using Slack, periodically reading the most recent message in a designated channel for commands. The variant Mandiant analysed encrypts its Slack messages with AES-256-CTR before Base64 encoding them, polls with a 150 to 450 second jitter interval, and supports four commands — `cmd`, `upload`, `download` and `sleep`.

Mandiant had seen the same backdoor deployed by the same actor in a separate campaign in early 2025, following exploitation of a different edge-appliance vulnerability. That reuse is the reason to model it rather than signature it: the file name, the channel and the workspace will change, and the one thing the operator cannot easily change is that a machine is doing the asking.

Slack is also the hardest kind of C2 destination to address with blocklists, because it is a service the organisation buys, allowlists and routes through its proxy without inspection. The only remaining discriminator is behavioural, and the timing distribution of a randomised sleep is a strong one.

## Why

- Reputation, domain age and certificate novelty are all useless here. The destination is the organisation's own collaboration platform, the TLS is valid, and the traffic traverses an allowlisted path. Any detection has to be built on how the traffic behaves rather than where it goes.
- A bounded-uniform jitter has a shape no human client produces. A hard floor at 150 seconds, a hard ceiling at 450, an even spread between them and no diurnal variation at all is a distribution that only a `sleep` call generates. Human Slack use clusters in working hours, collapses overnight and has a long tail; the beacon has none of those properties.
- Rule-based detection is the wrong tool for the specific reason that the false-positive set is large and legitimate. Many processes in an estate poll Slack on a schedule, so a threshold rule either alerts on all of them or is tuned until it alerts on none. Ranking turns that into a tractable work queue and produces, as a byproduct, the approved-automation register the organisation did not have.
- The workspace audit log gives an independent second view of the same beacon. An unapproved app or bot token reading one channel's history at a fixed cadence, in a channel with no human members, is detectable from the SaaS side with no network visibility at all — which matters because the host doing the polling was an appliance chosen for its lack of EDR.
