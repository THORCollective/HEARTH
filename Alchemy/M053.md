false_positive_notes: |
  Regular, automated Graph access is overwhelmingly legitimate and there will be a great deal of it. Backup and archiving products, e-discovery and compliance tooling, journaling and DLP integrations, migration tools, CRM and ticketing connectors, security products that read mail for phishing analysis, and monitoring or synthetic-transaction agents all authenticate app-only and all poll on a schedule. Several will poll frequently. So periodicity ALONE is not the finding, and a model that flags every regular caller is useless.
  Four features separate the shapes, and they should be combined rather than used singly. First, INTERVAL VARIANCE rather than interval length - legitimate schedulers are regular but jittered, because they are driven by cron-like timers with queueing, retries, backoff and variable work per cycle, so their inter-arrival distribution has a visible spread and often a long tail. An implant sleeping a fixed duration in a loop produces a distribution with near-zero coefficient of variation. Rank on CV, median absolute deviation or the height of the autocorrelation peak at the dominant lag, not on mean interval. Second, ABSENCE OF A DIURNAL OR WEEKLY ENVELOPE - legitimate integrations that serve user-facing workloads rise and fall with the business day and drop at weekends and holidays; an implant installed on a workstation beacons whenever the host is on, and an implant on a server beacons flat around the clock. A flat 24x7 profile with no weekend dip, from an app whose stated purpose is user-facing, is a strong discriminator. Third, SCOPE COMBINATION AND BREADTH - the reported design needs both a mailbox for commands and a drive for heartbeats and file transfer, so it holds both `Mail.*` and `Files.*` application permissions. Legitimate single-purpose integrations often hold one family or the other; a small, recently created, non-publisher-verified app holding both, estate-wide, is a narrow population worth reading by hand. Fourth, ACCESS SHAPE - a legitimate mail integration reads broadly across many mailboxes or a defined scoped set; the implant touches ONE operator-controlled mailbox folder repeatedly with a tight, repetitive URI pattern and essentially no breadth.
  Three cautions on building the model. Sampling and aggregation will destroy the signal - if the pipeline rolls Graph activity into five-minute or hourly buckets, a 10-second poll becomes a flat count and its variance disappears, so the model must run on raw per-request timestamps from `MicrosoftGraphActivityLogs`. Token caching decouples sign-ins from requests - a client-credentials token is valid for roughly an hour, so service principal SIGN-IN events appear about hourly regardless of how often the app actually calls Graph; periodicity must be measured on REQUESTS, with sign-in logs used only to establish that the identity is app-only. And a short observation window will produce false precision - two hours of data makes many callers look perfectly regular; use 30 days so that maintenance windows, deployment restarts and genuine schedule drift have a chance to appear in legitimate distributions.
detection_queries:
  - platform: Microsoft Sentinel / Defender XDR (KQL)
    description: Rank app-only Graph callers by how tightly their inter-arrival intervals cluster. Low coefficient of variation is the target; order ascending, not by volume. Derived from the reported cadences (Talos published no detection queries).
    query: |
      let window = 30d;
      MicrosoftGraphActivityLogs
      | where TimeGenerated > ago(window)
      | where isnotempty(AppId)
      | where RequestUri has_any ("/messages", "/mailFolders", "/drive", "/drives", "/items")
      | sort by AppId asc, TimeGenerated asc
      | extend PrevTime = prev(TimeGenerated), PrevApp = prev(AppId)
      | where AppId == PrevApp
      | extend Gap = datetime_diff('second', TimeGenerated, PrevTime)
      | where Gap between (1 .. 3600)
      | summarize Requests=count(), MedianGap=percentile(Gap, 50),
                  StdevGap=stdev(Gap), MeanGap=avg(Gap),
                  P10=percentile(Gap,10), P90=percentile(Gap,90),
                  DistinctDays=dcount(bin(TimeGenerated,1d))
                by AppId
      | where Requests > 500 and DistinctDays > 7
      | extend CoeffVar = StdevGap / MeanGap
      | extend SpreadRatio = todouble(P90) / todouble(P10)
      | where CoeffVar < 0.25 and SpreadRatio < 2.0
      | order by CoeffVar asc
  - platform: Microsoft Sentinel / Defender XDR (KQL)
    description: Diurnal-envelope test - a legitimate user-facing integration has a business-day shape; a flat 24x7 profile with no weekend dip does not.
    query: |
      let window = 30d;
      MicrosoftGraphActivityLogs
      | where TimeGenerated > ago(window)
      | where isnotempty(AppId)
      | summarize Requests=count() by AppId, Hour=hourofday(TimeGenerated),
                  IsWeekend=(dayofweek(TimeGenerated) in (0d, 6d))
      | summarize PeakHour=max(Requests), TroughHour=min(Requests),
                  WeekendReq=sumif(Requests, IsWeekend),
                  WeekdayReq=sumif(Requests, not(IsWeekend))
                by AppId
      | extend DiurnalRatio = todouble(PeakHour) / todouble(max_of(TroughHour, 1))
      | extend WeekendShare = todouble(WeekendReq) / todouble(max_of(WeekdayReq + WeekendReq, 1))
      | where DiurnalRatio < 2.0 and WeekendShare > 0.20
      | order by DiurnalRatio asc
  - platform: Microsoft Sentinel / Defender XDR (KQL)
    description: Narrow the ranked list to the reported capability shape - one app-only identity reaching both mail and drive resources, with very little breadth of access.
    query: |
      let window = 30d;
      MicrosoftGraphActivityLogs
      | where TimeGenerated > ago(window)
      | where isnotempty(AppId)
      | extend Surface = case(
            RequestUri has_any ("/messages", "/mailFolders", "/sendMail"), "Mail",
            RequestUri has_any ("/drive", "/drives", "/items", "/content"), "Drive",
            "Other")
      | where Surface != "Other"
      | summarize Surfaces=make_set(Surface), Requests=count(),
                  DistinctUris=dcount(RequestUri), DistinctTargets=dcount(tostring(UserId))
                by AppId
      | where array_length(Surfaces) == 2
      | where DistinctUris < 25
      | order by Requests desc
  - platform: Microsoft Graph / PowerShell (enrichment)
    description: Resolve each ranked AppId to the facts that decide the verdict - age, owner, publisher verification and the application permissions actually granted.
    query: |
      Connect-MgGraph -Scopes 'Application.Read.All','AuditLog.Read.All'
      $appIds = @('<AppId-from-ranked-list>')
      foreach ($id in $appIds) {
        $sp  = Get-MgServicePrincipal -Filter "appId eq '$id'"
        $app = Get-MgApplication -Filter "appId eq '$id'" -ErrorAction SilentlyContinue
        [pscustomobject]@{
          AppId            = $id
          DisplayName      = $sp.DisplayName
          CreatedDateTime  = $sp.AdditionalProperties.createdDateTime
          PublisherVerified= $sp.VerifiedPublisher.DisplayName
          SignInAudience   = $app.SignInAudience
          AppRoles         = ($sp.AppRoleAssignedTo | Measure-Object).Count
          Owners           = (Get-MgServicePrincipalOwner -ServicePrincipalId $sp.Id |
                               Select-Object -ExpandProperty Id) -join ','
          Credentials      = ($app.PasswordCredentials.Count, $app.KeyCredentials.Count) -join '/'
        }
      }
notes: |
  Platform: Microsoft 365 / Entra ID (Office Suite identity plane; the implant itself is Windows, covered by [[H354]]). Driven by Cisco Talos, "China-nexus UAT-11587 targets government and policy organizations across Asia with Antino backdoor" (September 30, 2026).
  The reported channel, exactly as published - Talos state that Antino "communicates exclusively through Microsoft 365, using the Microsoft Graph API to interact with Outlook and OneDrive as dead-drop C2 channels", and that this design "allows Antino's C2 traffic to blend into legitimate Microsoft application synchronization at the network layer". They name the endpoints - "outbound connections terminate at `graph.microsoft.com` and `login.microsoftonline.com`, both of which are widely trusted and commonly allowed in enterprise environments". On authentication - "the Antino Gen2 implant authenticates to Microsoft Graph using the OAuth 2.0 client-credentials flow. This authentication method allows the registered Entra ID application to access the configured Outlook mailbox and OneDrive resources without requiring an interactive user sign-in."
  The two mechanisms and their cadences - these are the numbers the model is built on. Mechanism 1, OneDrive file-based communication, uses three folder paths, named from the OPERATOR's perspective - `/antino/heartbeats/{id}.json` (implant upload, beacon/check-in carrying system state), `/antino_downloads/{file}` (implant upload, data taken from victims), and `/antino_uploads/{file}` (threat actor upload, toolkit delivery staging). The heartbeat JSON carries the session ID, timestamp, online/offline status, machine name, username, platform and a campaign code from the backdoor configuration, with a randomly generated UUID as the filename. Talos state the implant "uploads the heartbeat file to OneDrive during initial execution and RESENDS EVERY MINUTE". Mechanism 2, Outlook command communication - "the implant actively pulls commands from the threat actor's Outlook mailbox folder EVERY 10 SECONDS". Command messages are identified by the subject prefix `command_req_[session_id]` and responses by `command_res_[session_id]`. Message bodies carry JSON with `command_type`, `command_data` and `request_id`; responses add `success`, `result` and `error`.
  Why this is Alchemy and not Flames - the subject prefixes are a perfectly good string IOC and worth a retro-hunt, but they are configuration, not behaviour, and the next build changes them for free. Everything durable about this channel is statistical. The destination is Microsoft's own, so no reputation or TLS signal helps. The authentication is a supported, widely used flow. The volume is LOW, so any threshold tuned to catch bulk collection misses it entirely. What cannot be hidden without redesigning the implant is that a 10-second poll and a 60-second heartbeat produce an inter-arrival distribution with almost no variance, and that no business process has that shape. The hunt is therefore a ranking over a distribution, which is the Alchemy trigger - per-identity periodicity analysis where a static threshold would not generalise across tenants.
  Why rank on variance rather than interval or volume - a legitimate scheduler is regular but jittered. It is driven by timers with queueing, retries, exponential backoff and variable work per cycle, so its gaps spread out and it usually has a long tail. A sleep-in-a-loop implant does not. Coefficient of variation, median absolute deviation, or the autocorrelation peak height at the dominant lag all capture this; request COUNT does not, and mean interval does not either, because a legitimate app may well poll every 10 seconds on average while varying wildly around it. Order the output ascending by CV and read the top of the list.
  Why both surfaces matter for scoping - the design needs a mailbox for tasking and a drive for heartbeats and file transfer, so the service principal holds application permissions in both the `Mail.*` and `Files.*` families. That combination, on a recently created, non-publisher-verified app with few owners, is a small enough population in most tenants to enumerate by hand - which is why the third query narrows the ranked list rather than being a detection on its own.
  Two measurement traps that will silently defeat this hunt - FIRST, measure periodicity on REQUESTS, not on sign-ins. A client-credentials access token is valid for roughly an hour, so `AADServicePrincipalSignInLogs` will show an app-only identity authenticating about hourly no matter whether it then calls Graph twice or twenty thousand times. Sign-in logs establish that the identity is app-only; `MicrosoftGraphActivityLogs` establishes the cadence. SECOND, do not let the pipeline pre-aggregate. A 10-second poll bucketed into five-minute or hourly counts becomes a flat line and its variance - the entire signal - is gone. This hunt requires raw per-request timestamps, which is a prerequisite worth confirming before running it.
  Triage path on a ranked hit - resolve the `AppId` to its registration and ask the ordinary questions: when was it created, who owns it, is the publisher verified, what application permissions were granted and by whom (Entra audit `Consent to application` and `Add app role assignment to service principal`), and does any business owner recognise it. Then look at WHAT it touched - the reported implant hits one operator-controlled mailbox folder with a tight, repetitive URI pattern and almost no breadth, which is the opposite of a legitimate mail integration reading across many mailboxes. Finally, pivot to the endpoint via [[H354]] - Antino's persistence and PowerShell proxying are observable on the host even when the network channel is not.
  Companion artifacts from the same report, for retro-hunting rather than modelling - the Outlook subject prefixes `command_req_[session_id]` and `command_res_[session_id]`; the OneDrive paths `/antino/heartbeats/`, `/antino_uploads/` and `/antino_downloads/`; heartbeat filenames of the form `{session_id}.json` where the session ID is a UUID v4 (Gen2) or an XOR/Base64 encoding of process ID, computer name, username and platform (Gen1). Antino's command set includes `cmd`, `powershell`, `system_info`, `execute_program`, `list_files`, `upload_file`, `download_file`, `load_shellcode`, `add_to_run` and `exit`. Gen1 builds used `sendsession` and heartbeat email DRAFTS rather than OneDrive JSON, so a drafts-folder variant of the same cadence is in scope.
  Hardening that shrinks the attack surface this hunt watches - the channel depends on a registered Entra application holding broad app-only mail and file permissions. Inventory app-only identities and their application permissions; prefer scoped access (Application Access Policies for Exchange, which restrict an app's mailbox reach to a defined group) over tenant-wide `Mail.Read`; require publisher verification and an owner for every registration; apply Conditional Access for workload identities to restrict app-only sign-ins by IP where the integration's source is known; and alert on `Add app role assignment to service principal` for the `Mail.*` and `Files.*` families as a change-control event rather than discovering the grant during a hunt.
  Detection breadth beyond what the report attests - Talos documented the two mechanisms, the exact cadences, the folder paths, the subject prefixes, the authentication flow and the endpoints, and published no detection queries. The variance-ranking model, the diurnal-envelope test, the dual-surface scoping, the sign-in-versus-request measurement caution and the hardening list are this hunt's construction from those reported mechanics. The cadences, paths, prefixes, command set and flow are the attested artifacts.

# M053

Antino, the Rust backdoor Talos attributes to the China-nexus actor UAT-11587, runs its command and control entirely through Microsoft 365. It authenticates to Microsoft Graph with the OAuth 2.0 client-credentials flow — no interactive sign-in — and uses an operator-controlled Outlook mailbox for tasking and OneDrive for heartbeats and file transfer. Talos are explicit about why: the traffic terminates at `graph.microsoft.com` and `login.microsoftonline.com`, so it "blend[s] into legitimate Microsoft application synchronization at the network layer".

Every signal a network-centric detection would use is therefore neutralised by design. The destination is Microsoft's own, with Microsoft's certificate. The authentication flow is supported and widely used. The volume is low, so anything tuned to catch bulk collection sees nothing.

What the implant cannot hide is its clock. Talos report that the OneDrive heartbeat is resent **every minute**, and that the implant pulls commands from the Outlook folder **every 10 seconds**. That is a machine cadence, and it produces an inter-arrival distribution with almost no variance.

The subject prefixes Talos recovered — `command_req_[session_id]` and `command_res_[session_id]` — and the OneDrive paths `/antino/heartbeats/`, `/antino_uploads/` and `/antino_downloads/` are worth a retro-hunt. But they are configuration, and the next build changes them for nothing. The timing is architectural.

So this is a ranking problem rather than a rule. Model the per-service-principal request interval distribution over 30 days and sort app-only identities by how *tightly* their gaps cluster — coefficient of variation ascending, not request count descending. Legitimate schedulers are regular but jittered, because they carry queueing, retries, backoff and variable work per cycle; a sleep-in-a-loop implant is not. Then narrow with two more features: no diurnal or weekend envelope, and application permissions spanning both `Mail.*` and `Files.*`, which the design requires because it needs a mailbox and a drive.

Two measurement traps will quietly defeat this. Service principal sign-in logs show app-only identities authenticating roughly hourly regardless of real cadence, because a client-credentials token lasts about an hour — periodicity has to be measured on `MicrosoftGraphActivityLogs` requests, with sign-in logs used only to confirm the identity is app-only. And if the pipeline pre-aggregates into five-minute or hourly buckets, a 10-second poll flattens into a constant and the variance signal is gone entirely.

## Why

- Every non-temporal signal is neutralised on purpose, so timing is the only durable one left. The endpoint is Microsoft's, the auth flow is legitimate, and the volume is low — reputation, TLS inspection and volumetric thresholds all return nothing. Periodicity is a property of the implant's control loop rather than its configuration, which is what makes it survive a rebuild.
- Variance is the discriminator, and it is the one most beaconing analytics get wrong. Ranking by request volume or mean interval finds busy legitimate integrations; ranking by coefficient of variation finds the caller whose gaps barely move. Legitimate schedulers jitter because of queueing, retries and variable work per cycle, and that spread is the signal rather than noise to be smoothed away.
- A static threshold cannot generalise, which is precisely the Alchemy case. Every tenant has a different population of legitimate app-only integrations with different polling schedules, so "flag anything polling faster than N" is either empty or unusable depending on the tenant. A per-identity distribution ranked within the tenant adapts automatically.
- The capability shape narrows the candidate set to something a human can read. The design needs both a mailbox and a drive, so the service principal holds `Mail.*` and `Files.*` application permissions together — on a recently created, non-publisher-verified app that combination is a short list in most tenants, turning a statistical ranking into a handful of named registrations to adjudicate.
- The two measurement traps are the difference between this hunt working and producing a confident empty result. Measuring cadence on sign-ins instead of requests hides everything behind hourly token refresh, and any pre-aggregation destroys the variance outright. Surfacing both makes the prerequisite explicit rather than discovered after a clean run is believed.
- It leads somewhere concrete. A ranked hit resolves to an `AppId` with an owner, a creation date, a consent record and a permission grant — which is an answerable governance question, and it pivots straight to the endpoint behaviour in [[H354]].
