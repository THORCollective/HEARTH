title: "Scoring the post-theft inference call: a short-lived process that reads credential stores, then POSTs one oversized body to an LLM endpoint and retries against two different providers in seconds"
hypothesis: >-
  An infostealer that sends its haul to a commercial LLM for appraisal cannot be caught with a destination list, because the destination is a sanctioned AI provider that the organisation may deliberately allow. What it cannot hide is the shape of the transaction. In a window of seconds, one short-lived process reads browser credential stores and wallet paths, issues a single POST to an inference endpoint with a body one to two orders of magnitude larger than an interactive chat turn, and — if the model declines — repeats the same oversized request against a second and then a third provider before writing a zip and removing itself. Score processes on that conjunction: credential-store reads, one-shot oversized inference POST, cross-provider retry inside a short interval, archive write, self-delete. Any one factor is common; the conjunction is not, and it is not expressible as a static rule.

false_positive_notes: |
  Developer workstations generate the bulk of legitimate inference traffic and some of it is genuinely large: a long context window, a pasted log file, a repository summarisation call or a RAG request with retrieved documents can all produce a request body of several hundred kilobytes. Fit the size distribution per host class and per process, not fleet-wide, or every engineer running a coding agent will sit in the tail.
  Multi-provider traffic from one process is normal for exactly the tools this model is trying to distinguish from. Gateways, routers and agent frameworks fan out across providers by design, and `openrouter.ai` exists to do so. The discriminator is not that several providers were contacted — it is that the *same oversized body* was replayed to a second provider within seconds, following a refusal, with no success in between and no further traffic afterwards.
  Short process lifetime plus a self-delete is common in installers, updaters and build tooling. Alone it is near-useless as a signal; weighted into the conjunction it is one of the cheaper factors to compute.
  Reads of browser credential stores are the weakest factor in the model and should be weighted accordingly, because `DeviceFileEvents` records creates, modifies, renames and deletes but no reads. The usable proxies are the EDR's own credential-access behavioural signals and the file copies the stealer makes before decryption, not read events.
  A process that scores highly but resolves to a signed, sanctioned AI client is a tuning outcome, not a finding. Allowlist on the triple of signer, image path and destination, since the image name is the field an operator controls.
  Fitting the model on an estate that has never permitted inference traffic will produce a distribution with no body, and every call will look anomalous. In that case the hunt collapses to the much simpler question of why any process is reaching an inference endpoint at all, and the scoring adds nothing.
notes: |
  Platform - Windows.
  What the report states - Cisco Talos describes VOLTA-AUTODOXER, surfaced through the CAIRN research effort, as a malware that "is a self-deleting Windows infostealer that augments its post-theft workflow with an LLM-powered victim-profiling stage." The idea Talos names at the top of the report is the reason a destination list cannot work: "cast a wide net, steal everything, and let AI sort out the specifics."
  The call itself - the bundle is "serialized and POSTed to OpenRouter under a system prompt that instructs the model to act as" — in the report's words — "a forensic intelligence analyst drafting a target assessment." The configuration table gives the endpoint as `https[:]//openrouter[.]ai/api/v1/chat/completions`, the default model as `anthropic/claude-opus-4.6`, the fallback chain as `moonshotai/kimi-k2` then `google/gemini-2.5-flash`, the temperature as "0.2 — deterministic, consistent output", and the request format as "Hand-assembled JSON; no SDK; User-Agent Mozilla/5.0."
  That last field is the single most useful detection detail in the report, and it is the one this model weights most heavily after request size. A hand-assembled request with a browser User-Agent is not what any SDK produces: the official clients send their own identifiable agent strings. A POST to an inference endpoint carrying a generic `Mozilla/5.0` is a mismatch between the transport and the destination.
  The refusal failover - Talos records that the malware checks each response for refusal phrasing before moving on to the next model in a chain of up to three. The report's own conclusion draws out why this matters beyond this one family: "As AI integration in malware matures, refusal resilience may become a standard design requirement." For a hunter, refusal resilience is a gift, because it converts a single call into a short burst of near-identical oversized calls to *different* providers — a far rarer pattern than one call to one provider.
  Why this is Alchemy and not Flames - three reasons, and the first is decisive. The destination is legitimate and in many estates deliberately sanctioned, so no allow or deny list separates this from approved use; [[H409]] treats `openrouter.ai` as a *sanctioned* destination for exactly that reason. Second, the discriminating quantity is a distribution: request body size relative to what this process and this host class normally send. Third, the signal is a sequence with a timing constraint — reads, then one POST, then cross-provider repeats inside seconds — and sequence membership is what is being scored, not any individual event.
  Features to compute, per process instance - score and weight rather than threshold:
  - **Request body size percentile** for the inference POST, against the fitted per-host-class distribution. The evidence bundle Talos describes carries geolocation, employer, card last-4 digits, Slack workspaces, document authorship metadata and more; it is a document, not a chat turn.
  - **Distinct inference providers contacted within 120 seconds** by one process ID, with near-identical request sizes. Two is unusual, three is close to unique outside gateways and agent frameworks.
  - **User-Agent / destination mismatch**: a generic browser agent string on a POST to an inference API.
  - **Request count**: exactly one successful completion per provider attempt, with no conversational follow-up. Interactive and agentic use produces a series; this produces a single shot.
  - **Preceding credential-store activity** within the same process, weighted low for the telemetry reason in the false-positive notes.
  - **Process lifetime** under a few minutes, with the image removed afterwards.
  - **Archive write** in the same window. The report names three staged outputs; see the artefact list below.
  - **Signer state and image path**: unsigned, or running from a user-writable path.
  Candidate-generation query - this is the cheap pass that produces the population to score, not the detection itself:
  ```kql
  let inference = dynamic(["openrouter.ai", "api.openai.com", "api.anthropic.com",
                           "generativelanguage.googleapis.com", "api.moonshot.cn",
                           "api.deepseek.com", "api.mistral.ai", "api.groq.com"]);
  let sanctionedClients = dynamic(["cursor.exe", "code.exe", "chrome.exe", "msedge.exe",
                                   "firefox.exe", "ollama.exe", "python.exe", "node.exe"]);
  DeviceNetworkEvents
  | where Timestamp > ago(30d)
  | where isnotempty(RemoteUrl)
  | extend Host = tostring(parse_url(RemoteUrl).Host)
  | where Host in~ (inference)
  | summarize providers = dcount(Host), providerSet = make_set(Host, 8),
             calls = count(), firstCall = min(Timestamp), lastCall = max(Timestamp),
             urls = make_set(RemoteUrl, 8)
         by DeviceId, DeviceName, InitiatingProcessId, InitiatingProcessFileName,
            InitiatingProcessFolderPath, InitiatingProcessAccountName
  | where providers >= 2
  | extend burstSeconds = datetime_diff('second', lastCall, firstCall)
  | where burstSeconds <= 120
  | where InitiatingProcessFileName !in~ (sanctionedClients)
  | order by providers desc, burstSeconds asc
  ```
  `DeviceNetworkEvents` has no request-body-size column, which is why the size feature has to come from a proxy or TLS-inspecting gateway and why this query can only generate candidates. Where `RemoteUrl` is empty — common when only the TLS handshake is observed — fall back to `RemoteIP` and resolve it separately.
  Tying the burst to its process, with a bounded window. A join on `DeviceId` alone over thirty days correlates unrelated days and will manufacture pairs that never coexisted:
  ```kql
  let bursts = DeviceNetworkEvents
      | where Timestamp > ago(30d)
      | where isnotempty(RemoteUrl)
      | extend Host = tostring(parse_url(RemoteUrl).Host)
      | where Host has_any ("openrouter.ai", "api.openai.com", "api.anthropic.com",
                            "generativelanguage.googleapis.com")
      | summarize CallTime = min(Timestamp), providers = dcount(Host)
              by DeviceId, DeviceName, InitiatingProcessId
      | where providers >= 2;
  let archives = DeviceFileEvents
      | where Timestamp > ago(30d)
      | where ActionType in ("FileCreated", "FileRenamed")
      | where FileName endswith ".zip" or FileName endswith ".txt"
      | project ArchiveTime = Timestamp, DeviceId, FileName, FolderPath,
                InitiatingProcessId;
  bursts
  | join kind=inner (archives) on DeviceId, InitiatingProcessId
  | where ArchiveTime between (CallTime - 10m .. CallTime + 10m)
  | project CallTime, ArchiveTime, DeviceName, providers, FileName, FolderPath
  | order by CallTime desc
  ```
  Both `let` bindings are used, and the window is ten minutes either side of the first inference call, because the report describes the report file being assembled locally before the POST and the response being written after it.
  Named artefacts to look for alongside a high score - the locally assembled bundle is `Identity_Report.txt`, the returned payload is received as `AI_Analysis.txt`, and both leave together in `volta_dox.zip`. Talos describes three staged outputs: a `volta_log.zip` after the stealer runs, `volta_dox.zip` after the AI call, and a `volta_doxnet.zip` if the network enrichment produced anything. The report also names the forensic artefact worth preserving ahead of everything else — a timestamped harvest log in `%APPDATA%` — and explains why: "This is a timestamped harvest log and contains a record of exactly what was collected and whether the LLM call succeeded." On a host that scores highly, that file answers the scoping question directly.
  Sample details from the report - the sample hash is `57ef4242666d78c50df145b34023df564f0fad38812d6a580e66433d427e2279`, observed filenames are `9BY0tjwOc3ut.exe` and `yLP9m5J3i2Aw.exe`, the PE timestamp is given as 2026-08-17 20:35 and first seen as 2026-10-11 12:46. The filenames are random per build and are listed for completeness rather than as hunt inputs.
  Why there is nothing else to block - Talos is explicit: "All the observed endpoints for this malware are third-party controlled. There is no actor-owned infrastructure." There is no C2 domain, no actor IP and no hosting provider to report. The inference provider, a Telegram channel and a public valuation site are the entire egress surface, and all three are legitimate services.
  Scope boundary - the credential-theft legs of this family are already covered and this hunt deliberately does not restate them. Chrome App-Bound Encryption bypass and the `--headless` launch path are covered by existing hunts including [[H346]], [[H372]] and [[H216]]; wallet and seed-phrase collection by [[H284]], [[H322]] and [[M014]]; browser credential stores broadly by [[H247]]. What is new here is the appraisal call that follows them.
  Expected, not observed by Talos - the report does not give request or response body sizes, token counts, or the exact timing between failover attempts, so the size and interval features above must be fitted from the estate rather than seeded with values from the report. Talos does not state how the refusal check behaves on a non-refusal error such as a rate limit or an invalid key, so a burst that ends after two providers should not be assumed to mean the second call succeeded. The report also does not say the inference call is retried on a later run if all three models decline; it notes only the "ability to run without the LLM stage altogether if no key is present."
  One assessment worth carrying into the write-up - the inference round-trip happens on the victim machine and over the victim network connection before anything is exfiltrated. That means the appraisal traffic is visible to the organisation's own egress telemetry at the moment it happens, which is the one structural advantage defenders have against this design and the reason the hunt is worth running on live egress rather than only in retrospect.
  Closest existing coverage - [[M049]] is the nearest hunt and also scores multi-provider LLM traffic from a single process, but the behaviour is different in kind: CLOSEDQUORUM polls four providers *every cycle* and executes a plurality verdict, so its signature is a recurring fan-out with substantively identical prompts. This models a one-shot oversized call with sequential failover triggered by refusal, occurring once per victim after theft and never again. The distinction is cadence and intent: a standing consensus channel versus a single post-theft appraisal. [[H409]] hunts an AI coding agent whose configured inference endpoint was repointed to an attacker host, and treats `openrouter.ai` as sanctioned — the inverse framing of this hunt.

# M064

The AI layer in VOLTA is not doing anything a defender would normally call malicious. It is drafting a report. Cisco Talos describes the malware as one that "is a self-deleting Windows infostealer that augments its post-theft workflow with an LLM-powered victim-profiling stage", and the framing at the top of the report is the part that should worry anyone maintaining an egress policy: "cast a wide net, steal everything, and let AI sort out the specifics."

The bundle that goes out holds geolocation and home address, employer and employer domain, inferred birth year, card last-4 digits, Slack workspaces, document authorship metadata, and a live Roblox account valuation, and it is POSTed to OpenRouter under a system prompt instructing the model to act as "a forensic intelligence analyst drafting a target assessment." The endpoint is `https[:]//openrouter[.]ai/api/v1/chat/completions`, which many organisations deliberately permit.

So destination-based detection is structurally unavailable here, and Talos closes the door on indicator-based detection too: "All the observed endpoints for this malware are third-party controlled. There is no actor-owned infrastructure." No C2 domain, no actor IP, nothing to blocklist.

What is left is shape, and the shape is unusual in three ways that compose into something rare. The request is a serialized dossier rather than a chat turn, so its body is far outside the size distribution of ordinary inference traffic from the same host class. The request format is, in the report's words, "Hand-assembled JSON; no SDK; User-Agent Mozilla/5.0" — a browser agent string on an API call that no official client would produce. And the refusal handling converts one call into a burst: the malware checks each response for refusal phrasing and moves on through a chain of up to three models, with the fallback chain given as `moonshotai/kimi-k2` then `google/gemini-2.5-flash`. One process replaying a near-identical oversized body to three different providers inside a couple of minutes, once, and then never again, is not a pattern legitimate tooling produces.

That is a scoring problem rather than a rule. Each factor on its own is noisy — engineers send large contexts, gateways fan out across providers, installers self-delete — and the conjunction, weighted and fitted to the estate, is what separates an appraisal call from an afternoon of coding assistance.

## Why

- No list can express this. The destination is a legitimate AI provider that many estates sanction on purpose, and there is no actor-owned infrastructure anywhere in the chain, so both allowlists and blocklists are inapplicable by construction.
- The discriminating quantity is a distribution, not a value. A serialized victim dossier sits in the far tail of inference request sizes for its host class, and the tail boundary differs between an engineering fleet and a finance department.
- Refusal resilience is a detection opportunity. Designing around model guardrails turns a single call into a short cross-provider burst with near-identical bodies, which is a much rarer event than any single call to any single provider.
- The transport betrays the author. A hand-assembled request with a generic browser User-Agent reaching an inference API is a mismatch no SDK produces, and it costs one feature to compute.
- The traffic is visible at the moment of use. The inference round-trip runs on the victim machine over the victim network before anything is exfiltrated, so this is one of the few stealer behaviours an organisation can see live rather than reconstruct afterwards.
- The pattern generalises past this family. Talos frames effort displacement as a trend rather than a one-off and expects refusal resilience to become standard, which means the scored shape — oversized one-shot call, cross-provider retry, no follow-up — will outlive the specific endpoint, model names and filenames in this report.
