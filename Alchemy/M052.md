  Base64-shaped request bodies are extremely common and this model will find a great deal of legitimate traffic: JWTs and OAuth assertions, SAML responses, session cookies, CSRF tokens, WebSocket and SignalR frames, embedded images in form posts, protobuf and MessagePack payloads wrapped in Base64, browser telemetry and crash reports, RUM beacons, antivirus and EDR telemetry uploads, software licence checks, and virtually every SaaS analytics SDK. notes: |
  Platform: Network (HTTP/HTTPS egress), Windows endpoints as the traffic source. Driven by Volexity, "Mind the (Patch) Gap, Part 2: Fake Websites Used to Deploy Chrome & Windows 0-Day Exploits" (September 21, 2026), which documents the CLEANGULP implant used by UTA0565 encrypting C2 traffic with AES-256-GCM and encoding it with Base64 over a custom alphabet: `3GHIJKLMNOPQRSTUb4Fcd0fghijklmnopq/rstuvwxyzABCDEWXYZ12V56789a+e`. The AES key is the SHA-256 of that alphabet string (`cbeeb7dd5e89261cde032825fd10bb80bad2e3fbf5b91fdc9137ad463ffa8f21`), and the initial beacon is `POST /beacon/pre-register` with `Content-Type: application/octet-stream` to `thecovnresation[.]com` over plain HTTP. Volexity published no detection queries; this model is derived from the reported encoding scheme.
  Why a permuted alphabet is the interesting case - Substituting the 64 symbols of the Base64 alphabet costs the developer nothing — it is a lookup-table swap — and it breaks a surprising amount of defensive tooling: signatures matching standard Base64 character classes, decoders in sandboxes and proxies that attempt automatic Base64 expansion, YARA rules keyed on Base64-encoded strings, and any analyst pipeline that pipes suspicious blobs through `base64 -d`. What it cannot change is the *shape* of the output. A block encoder emitting 6 bits per symbol from a 64-symbol set produces a specific and recognisable statistical profile no matter which 64 symbols are chosen, and because the plaintext here is AES-GCM ciphertext, the underlying bytes are indistinguishable from random — which makes the resulting character distribution nearly uniform and therefore unusually easy to characterise.
  Feature engineering, per request body (or per destination per hour, aggregated) - (1) Symbol-set cardinality: count of distinct characters in the body. The signature is a value clustering just below or at 64 for bodies of reasonable length, plus one or two padding characters. Bodies under roughly 200 bytes will not exhibit full cardinality by chance, so gate on length before computing this. (2) Alphabet identity: the actual set of characters observed, compared against the RFC 4648 standard alphabet and the URL-safe variant. The high-value feature is a 64-symbol set of the right size whose membership differs from both — CLEANGULP's alphabet retains `/` and `+` but permutes everything else, so membership overlap will be high while *ordering* differs, which is why set membership plus positional frequency, not membership alone, is the discriminator. (3) Normalised character-frequency entropy: Shannon entropy of the character distribution divided by log2(cardinality). Encoded ciphertext sits at 0.98-1.00; encoded JSON, protobuf or text sits measurably lower because the plaintext has structure that survives encoding. This single feature separates encoded-ciphertext from encoded-structured-data better than any other. (4) Chi-square goodness-of-fit against uniform over the observed symbol set — a formal version of (3) that yields a p-value and is more stable on short bodies. (5) Length congruence: body length modulo 4 equal to 0, and the distribution of lengths across many requests. An encoder emits 4-symbol groups; a beacon protocol with fixed-size fields produces a tight, multimodal length distribution — Volexity records a 100-byte initial beacon — whereas legitimate variable payloads spread widely. (6) Padding behaviour: presence, position and rate of a trailing padding character, which should occur at the theoretically expected frequency for random input lengths. (7) Absence of structure: no JSON, XML, form-encoding or protobuf field markers, and no dictionary words on any trivial decode attempt. (8) Metadata companions (usable without body visibility, lower precision on their own): `Content-Type: application/octet-stream` on a POST, a URI path with a beacon-like structure, a static or browser-mimicking `User-Agent` mismatched to the process, plain HTTP rather than HTTPS, and a session identifier that is UUID-shaped but not RFC 9562-compliant — CLEANGULP used time-based pseudo-UUIDs such as `146c38a3-65fa-3bf5-4030-67192b000bc0`, whose version nibble is invalid, and checking UUID version and variant bits is a cheap, precise, nearly free test.
  Modelling approach - Treat this as scoring and ranking, not classification, and aggregate per destination rather than per request. Compute the features per body, then summarise per (destination host, URI path prefix) per day: median normalised entropy, modal cardinality, fraction of bodies with length ≡ 0 mod 4, alphabet-identity flag, request count and distinct source-host count. Rank destinations by a composite of high uniformity, 64-cardinality, non-standard alphabet and low estate prevalence. A simple weighted score is sufficient and strongly preferable to a learned classifier here, because the features are individually interpretable and analyst trust matters more than marginal accuracy — and because there are no labels. Layer beaconing regularity (inter-request interval variance from `conn.log`) as an independent multiplier; encoding shape plus periodicity is far stronger evidence than either alone. Enrich the top of the ranking with domain age, certificate age, ASN and whether the destination typosquats a known brand, which is how UTA0565's infrastructure presented.
  Validation and tuning - Seed with synthetic positives: generate AES-GCM ciphertext, encode it with several permuted alphabets, POST it to a lab endpoint, and confirm it ranks at the top. Then verify the model does *not* rank JWT, SAML, protobuf-in-Base64 and image-upload traffic highly — if it does, the normalised-entropy feature is underweighted relative to cardinality, which is the most common tuning error since structured plaintext also produces full cardinality at length. Measure precision at the top 20 destinations per week; expect the ranking to be dominated initially by legitimate telemetry endpoints, and convert each cleared destination into an inventory entry rather than a filter rule.
  Coverage honesty - CLEANGULP's own C2 ran over plain HTTP, which is convenient, but the technique does not require it. This model's reach is bounded by request-body visibility: full coverage where TLS is inspected, metadata-only features elsewhere. State that bound explicitly when reporting results so a clean output is not mistaken for an absence of the behaviour.
  Cross-technique: pair with [[H347]] for the Mark-of-the-Web removal step in this same UTA0565 chain, and with [[M049]] for HEARTH's existing web-protocol C2 modelling.

# M052

Volexity's September 21, 2026 report on UTA0565 includes an unusually complete description of the CLEANGULP implant's command-and-control encoding. Traffic is encrypted with AES-256-GCM and then Base64-encoded — but not with the standard alphabet. The implant uses:

`3GHIJKLMNOPQRSTUb4Fcd0fghijklmnopq/rstuvwxyzABCDEWXYZ12V56789a+e`

The same string, SHA-256 hashed, is the AES key. The initial beacon is a 100-byte `POST /beacon/pre-register` with `Content-Type: application/octet-stream`, sent over plain HTTP to a typosquatted domain.

Permuting the Base64 alphabet is nearly free for the developer and disproportionately effective against defenders. It breaks signatures written against standard Base64 character classes, defeats the automatic Base64 decoding that sandboxes, proxies and analyst tooling apply opportunistically, invalidates YARA rules keyed on Base64-encoded strings, and ensures that anything piped through `base64 -d` returns garbage — which reads as "not Base64" rather than "Base64 with a different alphabet." The alphabet is also per-campaign, so even publishing this one buys little.

What permutation cannot change is the statistical shape of the output. An encoder emitting 6 bits per symbol produces a 64-symbol set whose members appear with characteristic frequencies and whose output length is congruent to 4-symbol groups, regardless of which 64 symbols were chosen. Because the plaintext here is AES-GCM ciphertext — effectively random bytes — the resulting character distribution is very close to uniform, which is both distinctive and easy to measure. That combination is what makes this a model-assisted hunt: no single request is suspicious, no indicator is stable, and the signal only exists as a distribution over a destination's traffic.

## Why

- The evasion targets exactly the layer most organisations rely on, and the residual signal sits one layer down. Signature and decode-based tooling fails completely against a permuted alphabet, while the encoding's statistical fingerprint survives untouched — so moving the detection from symbol identity to symbol-set cardinality and uniformity restores coverage without needing the alphabet.
- Normalised character-frequency entropy cleanly separates the case that matters. Encoded ciphertext approaches perfect uniformity, whereas encoded JSON, protobuf or text retains enough plaintext structure to measure lower — which is what distinguishes an encrypted C2 channel from the enormous volume of legitimate Base64 an enterprise emits every day.
- Several features are effectively free and independently precise. Checking whether a session identifier's UUID version and variant bits are RFC 9562-compliant costs nothing and caught CLEANGULP's time-based pseudo-UUIDs outright; body length congruent to 4 and a tight multimodal length distribution are similarly cheap arithmetic on data already logged.
- Aggregating per destination rather than per request is what makes the output actionable. Individual encoded bodies are unremarkable, but a single low-prevalence, recently-registered destination receiving uniformly-distributed 64-symbol bodies from one host on a regular interval is a small, triageable list — and combining encoding shape with beaconing periodicity is far stronger than either signal alone.
- The false positives are inventory work with lasting value. The destinations that rank highest before tuning are legitimate telemetry, analytics and identity endpoints, and cataloguing which of them receive encoded payloads produces an egress inventory most organisations lack and that every subsequent C2 hunt benefits from.
