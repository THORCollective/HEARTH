     Test-NetConnection, Resolve-DnsName. Baseline these alongside the binaries or the baseline has a hole an
     operator can walk through.

  - Discovery immediately followed by TCP probing of a domain controller on 445/3389, or by browser credential-store

# B034

The operator's entire discovery phase in the Deception.Pro report was `ipconfig /all`, `arp -a`, `net view`, `netstat`, `systeminfo` — five commands over ten minutes on 2026-07-28. Every one of them ships with Windows. Every one of them is run by helpdesk staff, login scripts and inventory agents thousands of times a day in any real environment. Writing a rule on any of them produces an unusable alert stream, which is presumably why HEARTH had no T1016 coverage at all.

The thing that was actually anomalous was not present in any single event. It was that five different discovery utilities ran inside one logon session inside ten minutes, from a user who had never run four of them. That is a claim you can only make if someone has already written down what normal looks like.

## Why

- **T1016 is the clearest case in ATT&CK where a baseline is the only viable approach.** The commands are built-in, undetectable by reputation, and legitimately used by the exact populations — helpdesk, admins, agents — that generate the most volume. There is no IOC and no suspicious binary. Either you have a per-role profile or you have nothing.
- **The burst metric is what converts noise into signal.** One `ipconfig` is meaningless. Five distinct discovery utilities in one logon session in ten minutes is a human orienting themselves in an environment they do not know. Baselining "distinct utilities per LogonId per 10 minutes" gives a single tunable number that captures hands-on-keyboard reconnaissance across the whole T1016/T1018/T1046/T1082 family at once.
- **The per-account rarity list is usually the surprise.** Most organisations assume network discovery is ubiquitous. When you actually count, the set of standard (non-IT) accounts that have run `arp -a` or `net view` in ninety days is typically tiny — and that list is immediately actionable without any threshold tuning at all.
- **Discovery is the earliest reliable point of hands-on-keyboard detection.** It happens after execution but before lateral movement, credential dumping and exfiltration. In the source intrusion the discovery burst preceded DC port-probing, domain-admin enumeration, browser credential theft and upload to `temp[.]sh`. Catching the session at minute ten of the discovery phase is a meaningfully different incident than catching it after the archive leaves.
- **Baselining the PowerShell equivalents is not optional.** `Get-NetIPConfiguration` and `Get-NetNeighbor` do the same job as `ipconfig` and `arp` and produce no matching process-name event. A baseline built only on the classic binaries leaves a documented, trivially-used gap.
