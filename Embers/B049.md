false_positive_notes: |
  This is an inventory exercise, so the question is what belongs in the register rather than what to suppress, and several things will look alarming on first collection while being entirely routine. SharePoint and OneDrive "Open with Explorer" / mapped-library workflows start the WebClient service legitimately and will dominate the volume anywhere they are in use — these are the single most important population to identify early, because their presence or absence determines the whole detection posture downstream. Logon scripts and GPO-delivered software frequently invoke `rundll32` against DLLs on internal file servers; these are stable, they point into your own namespace, and they belong in the allowlist by destination host rather than by command line. Printer and scanner drivers, some backup agents, and a handful of enterprise applications mount UNC paths as part of normal operation. Expect also to find genuine legacy: a forgotten WebDAV-based intranet, an old document workflow, or a vendor appliance whose client mounts a share — these are exactly the findings that make the baseline worth doing, because each one is a standing exception somebody has to own. Two cautions on interpretation. First, an empty WebClient result is ambiguous and must be resolved: it may mean the service genuinely never starts, or it may mean service state changes are not being collected, and those lead to opposite conclusions. Verify collection explicitly on a host where you have deliberately triggered a WebDAV mount. Second, do not allowlist by the requesting process — `rundll32` is the thing being abused — allowlist by DESTINATION, because the durable distinction between benign and malicious here is internal-and-known versus external-and-arbitrary.
notes: |
  Collection window - 30 days, which covers a full monthly cycle including patch Tuesday, month-end finance processes and any monthly scheduled jobs that mount shares. The sanctioned-platform inventory is point-in-time and should be gathered on day one from SharePoint, OneDrive and document-management owners rather than accumulated from logs.
  Fields to collect, WebClient service - for every `WebClient` start across the estate - `hostname`, `timestamp`, the triggering user session, and whether the host is a workstation, server or VDI instance. Produce a per-host and per-business-unit frequency distribution. The headline number this baseline exists to produce is the PERCENTAGE OF HOSTS ON WHICH WEBCLIENT EVER STARTS. If that number is near zero, [[H313]] can use service start as a primary filter; if it is material, that condition must be dropped and replaced by destination scoping, and the baseline should say so explicitly.
  Fields to collect, mount destinations - for every WebDAV mount and every UNC access - `destination host`, `share path`, `requesting user`, `requesting process`, `internal or external`, and the resolved IP. Classify each destination host as (a) internal and sanctioned, (b) internal and unexplained, or (c) external. Category (c) should be empty or nearly so in a healthy environment, and every member of it is a finding in its own right.
  Fields to collect, remote-DLL execution - every process creation where `CommandLine` contains a UNC prefix and `Image` is one of `rundll32.exe`, `regsvr32.exe`, `mshta.exe`, `msiexec.exe`, `installutil.exe` or `certutil.exe`. Record `Image`, full `CommandLine`, `ParentImage`, `ParentCommandLine`, `User`, destination host, target filename, target EXTENSION, and whether execution was by named export or by ORDINAL. Two distributions matter for tuning the Flames hunt - how often a non-`.dll` extension appears (expected - almost never) and how often ordinal invocation appears (expected - rare and confined to a few in-box operations).
  Fields to collect, Run dialog usage - profile `RunMRU` values across the estate - the distinct strings, their frequency, and how many contain a command SEPARATOR (`&&`, `;`, `|`), a scripting engine, or a UNC path. This establishes what a normal Run-dialog entry looks like in this organisation, which is what makes a pasted ClickFix chain stand out later. In most estates the legitimate vocabulary is a few dozen short tokens.
  Immediate flags, do not wait for the baseline to complete - (1) any `rundll32`/`regsvr32`/`mshta` command line containing a UNC path whose destination host is EXTERNAL; (2) any remote-DLL execution where the target file has a non-`.dll` extension or is invoked by ordinal; (3) a `WebClient` service start on a domain controller, a server, or any host in a business unit with no sanctioned WebDAV workflow; (4) a `RunMRU` entry containing a multi-command chain with a scripting engine or UNC path; (5) WebDAV `PROPFIND`/`OPTIONS` traffic to a registrable domain outside the organisation, especially with randomised subdomains under one parent.
  Deliverable - four artefacts, all diffable. (1) A WEBDAV POSTURE STATEMENT - the percentage of hosts on which WebClient ever starts, the sanctioned workflows that require it, and an explicit recommendation of whether service start is usable as a detection primary in this estate. (2) A DESTINATION ALLOWLIST - every internal host legitimately mounted over UNC or WebDAV, with owning application and business owner. (3) A REMOTE-EXECUTION REGISTER - every recurring `rundll32`/`regsvr32`/`mshta`-from-UNC invocation, with its source (logon script, GPO, application) and an owner. (4) A RUN-DIALOG PROFILE - the normal RunMRU vocabulary and the threshold above which a pasted string is anomalous. Re-snapshot at the end of the window to produce the first diff.

# B049

The WebDAV execution chain in [[H313]] — `pushd` to an attacker share, `rundll32` by ordinal, `popd` — has one condition that is either excellent or useless depending on facts about the environment that nobody usually knows: whether the `WebClient` service ever starts. WebClient is manual-start, exists solely to support WebDAV, and in a large share of enterprises never runs at all, which makes a single start event close to a standalone lead. But in organisations that use SharePoint mapped libraries, OneDrive "Open with Explorer", or a WebDAV-based document-management product, it starts all day long and the condition has to be discarded entirely in favour of destination scoping. Discovering which world you are in during an incident is too late. The same applies to remote-DLL execution: `rundll32` against a UNC path is rare in most estates and routine in a few that push software through logon scripts. This baseline settles both questions in advance and produces the allowlists that make the Flames hunt operational — and it does so with telemetry most environments already have.

## Why

- **It decides the detection posture rather than merely tuning it.** This is not a threshold to adjust; it is a binary architectural choice between two different hunts. Either WebClient start is a primary filter or destination classification is, and running the Flames hunt without having made that choice deliberately produces either an alert flood or a silently ineffective query.
- **External UNC destinations are the durable discriminator and they are directly enumerable.** Legitimate WebDAV and remote-DLL activity is overwhelmingly internal, to hosts in the organisation's own namespace. Building the internal allowlist once means every future hit can be judged on destination alone — a property that survives the campaign's filenames, domains and ordinals rotating, because the internal-versus-external distinction is a fact about the organisation rather than about the malware.
- **It surfaces standing exposure that no alert would ever raise.** Forgotten WebDAV intranets, legacy document workflows and vendor clients that mount shares are all discovered by this exercise and by nothing else. Each is both a tuning input and a risk item with an owner, so the baseline pays for itself even if the associated threat never materialises.
- **The Run-dialog profile is the piece nobody has and ClickFix depends on.** ClickFix, and the whole family of paste-the-command lures, requires a human to enter a long command into the Run dialog. `RunMRU` records it, the legitimate vocabulary in any organisation is a few dozen short tokens, and knowing that vocabulary is what turns a pasted chain from an unremarkable registry value into an obvious outlier.
