false_positive_notes: |
  The Nostr protocol itself has essentially no enterprise population, which makes the network condition unusually clean — in a corporate macOS fleet, connections to `api.nostr.watch` or to public Nostr relays such as `relay.damus.io` or `nos.lol` have no business justification and can be treated as anomalous on sight. The realistic benign matches are individuals running personal Nostr clients (Damus, Amethyst) on a corporate device, or security researchers studying the protocol; both are resolved by asking rather than by tuning, and both will show an interactive client application rather than a `nohup`-launched binary as the connecting process. The `--type=renderer` argument is where care is genuinely required, because it is the single most common argument in a modern macOS process table: every Electron application — Slack, Teams, VS Code, Cursor, Discord, Notion — spawns renderer children with exactly that flag, and they are numerous and constant. The argument alone is worthless as a signal. What is anomalous is the COMBINATION of that argument with an executable that is not inside a legitimate `.app` bundle, is not signed by the corresponding vendor's team ID, and was launched via `nohup` rather than by the parent Electron framework. That combination is essentially absent from normal operation, and the implant's choice of this masquerade is precisely a bet that defenders filter on the argument and stop there. LaunchAgent creation has a moderate benign population — legitimate software installs agents routinely — so profile by whether the referenced binary is signed, notarised, and inside an `.app` bundle. Note that `loginwindow.plist` is a deliberately confusing filename choice, since `loginwindow` is a real Apple process; there is no legitimate `~/Library/LaunchAgents/loginwindow.plist` shipped by Apple, so that exact path is a direct hit. Finally, `xattr -rd com.apple.quarantine` does have legitimate developer use, which is why it belongs as a correlating condition rather than a standalone one.

# H316

The second half of the TraderTraitor intrusion SentinelOne documented is a command-and-control design that removes the domain from the equation. ROOFDECK, a Rust ARM64 macOS backdoor, finds its operator on the Nostr network: it pulls the live relay list from `api.nostr[.]watch/v1/online`, combines it with a hardcoded set of well-known public relays, connects over WebSocket, and looks up the operator's profile by public key. Commands are signed server-side with the operator's private key and verified against an embedded public key before execution, so possession of the relay confers nothing. Nostr relays are legitimate infrastructure carrying thousands of unrelated users' traffic — there is no attacker domain to sinkhole, no certificate to flag, and no server to seize, while the operator can migrate between relays at will because the implant follows the key rather than the address. Its sibling FLATROOF exfiltrates over a Telegram bot token. Both masquerade as Electron renderer children (`--type=renderer`) from Apple-impersonating directories under `~/Library`, and persist via `~/Library/LaunchAgents/loginwindow.plist`.

## Why

- **Key-addressed C2 breaks the assumption that blocking infrastructure disrupts an operator.** Every response playbook step — block the domain, sinkhole the IP, revoke the certificate, request a takedown — assumes the adversary is reachable at an address you can deny. Here the rendezvous is a public key on a decentralised network, so denying any individual relay costs the operator nothing and the implant simply finds them elsewhere.
- **The renderer masquerade is chosen precisely to exploit how defenders filter.** `--type=renderer` is among the most common arguments on any modern Mac because every Electron app produces them in volume, so an analyst's instinct is to exclude it wholesale. Adding the bundle, signature and team-ID conditions costs almost nothing and converts the adversary's best camouflage into one of the most specific conditions available.
- **Apple-impersonating directories under `~/Library` are a generic, durable tell.** Apple does not place executable Mach-O binaries directly inside `~/Library/com.apple.*` directories. That makes the pattern — rather than the three specific paths this campaign used — a standing detection that survives renaming, and one that can be swept across a fleet cheaply with a file inventory.
- **The Cursor-gated beaconing defeats interval analysis in a way worth knowing in advance.** Because the implants only beacon while the IDE runs, their traffic has a human, bursty, working-hours shape rather than a regular heartbeat — the exact profile beacon-detection models are tuned to dismiss as benign. Knowing that steers the hunt to process identity and persistence artefacts instead of wasting effort on timing analysis that is structurally unable to see this.
