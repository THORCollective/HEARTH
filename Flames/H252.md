# H252

Microsoft's CaptiveCrunch report (2026-07-31) describes something that inverts the usual drive-by assumption. The victim did not browse to a bad site and did not click a phishing link. They joined hotel Wi-Fi, and **Windows itself made the first request** — the built-in Network Connectivity Status Indicator probe that every machine fires to work out whether it is behind a captive portal. Storm-2945 had compromised the portal management systems, so that probe was answered by their infrastructure, which returned a convincing fake update page.

That is worth hunting specifically because the entry point is a mechanism defenders normally treat as background noise. HEARTH had no coverage referencing NCSI or captive portals at all.

## Why

- **The delivery trigger is an OS behaviour, not a user action.** Awareness training tells people not to click links in email; it says nothing about the connectivity check their laptop fires automatically on joining a network. The first attacker-controlled byte arrives before the user has decided to do anything.
- **The temporal join survives infrastructure churn.** Storm-2945's domains and IPs are already in the report and will be dead soon. The pattern — connectivity probe, then a new executable within two minutes, written by a browser — does not rotate, which is what makes this a hunt rather than an IOC sweep.
- **The blast radius is concentrated in exactly the population you care about.** Captive portals mean hotels, conference centres and airports, which means travelling staff: executives, sales, and anyone at an industry event. Midnight Blizzard's targeting of that group is deliberate, and it gives the hunt a natural scoping that keeps the false-positive load manageable.
- **The follow-on is full credential compromise, not just an implant.** ChocoShell pulls browser cookies, App-Bound-Encryption-bypassed passwords, M365/Entra access and refresh tokens, WAM tokens, and Wi-Fi keys via `netsh wlan show profile key=clear`. A single hit here means assuming identity compromise, not just reimaging a laptop.
