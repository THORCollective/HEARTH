false_positive_notes: |
  Network changes: Wi-Fi to cellular handoff, travel, and hotel or conference networks do change ASN in the middle of a session. Require the later leg to come from a hosting/VPN ASN or an ASN never seen for the user, together with a change in device ID or user agent family. Legitimate handoffs keep the same device ID and client.
  Corporate VPN, SASE, and split tunneling: allowlist corporate egress ASNs and documented personal VPN use before scoring ASN hops.
  Token-claim MFA by itself is normal: `MFA requirement satisfied by claim in the token` appears on almost every non-interactive refresh. Never alert on it without the ASN hop and a persistence write.
  New phone or laptop setup: legitimate device and Authenticator registration usually follows a fresh interactive MFA completion from the user's usual ASN, and the device usually goes on to MDM enrollment and a compliance record. A bare `Workplace` registration that never enrolls is the suspicious case.
  Helpdesk resets: `Admin registered security info` and `Admin deleted security info` carry an admin initiator. Correlate with ticketing and exclude them unless the admin account itself shows the replay pattern.

Keep only pairs where the later ASN has no sign-in history for the user in the previous 30 days or belongs to a hosting/VPN provider. Pivot: hunt forward on the new `device ID`. Later sign-ins carry a device claim and may move to a commercial VPN or a second hosting ASN (Huntress saw ExpressVPN and CrownCloud after registration). Strong red flags: a device OS or naming pattern that does not exist in the managed fleet; a device that is registered but never shows up in MDM or compliance records.
  Entra ID audit logs (Authentication Methods / UserManagement) - Core filter: self-initiated `User deleted security info`, `User registered security info`, or `User changed default security info` within 24h of the replay leg. Triage values: `method type` (Authenticator app, phone, OATH token), `initiated by` (user vs admin), `initiator IP address`, `target user`. Correlation: initiator ASN/IP matches the replay leg or the VPN ASN used after device registration; deletes come before registrations in one burst. Strong red flags: all prior methods deleted and one new Authenticator registration from an ASN outside the user's baseline; a new phone method with a country code that differs from every existing method.
  Microsoft 365 Unified Audit Log (Exchange Online) - Core filter: `New-InboxRule`, `Set-InboxRule`, `UpdateInboxRules`, or Graph `messageRules` writes where the rule moves mail to `RSS Feeds`, `RSS Subscriptions`, `Conversation History`, or `Archive`, or sets `DeleteMessage` or `MarkAsRead` to `True`. Post-compromise pivot: `MailItemsAccessed`, `SearchQueryInitiatedExchange`, and `Send` from the same session or ASN, to scope mailbox reading, invoice/payment searches, and outbound BEC or internal phishing. Strong red flags: rule names of one or two characters; keywords such as `invoice`, `payment`, `wire`, or the names and domains of the user's external correspondents.

# H411

On October 22, 2026, Huntress reported a ten-week Microsoft 365 campaign (July 14 to September 22, 2026) that it calls token spraying. It works like password spraying, except the attacker cycles through stolen session tokens instead of guessed passwords. Sessions were captured after victims passed MFA on AiTM relay pages, then replayed from an IP block owned by the German hosting provider 1337 Services GmbH. Huntress counted more than 200,000 sign-in attempts, 497 successful sign-ins, and 79 compromised organizations. In one case only four minutes passed between capture and replay. Entra ID Protection separately flagged several of the replay addresses. Huntress linked a set of replay sign-ins to a bare `node` user agent replaying tokens through the Microsoft Office client. After getting in, the attacker kept access in three ways: registering at least 48 rogue Entra devices, wiping and replacing victims' MFA methods, and creating inbox rules to hide malicious communications. After registering a rogue device from the 1337 Services address, the attacker often switched to ExpressVPN or CrownCloud egress. Huntress points out that a password reset alone leaves the attacker's device and MFA methods in place. It recommends Conditional Access that requires MFA for all users, all cloud apps, and all client app types, and says containment must also remove attacker-registered devices and MFA methods. Microsoft's AiTM/BEC reporting describes the same order of events: a replayed session cookie, MFA method changes made without a new challenge, then mailbox rules and follow-on phishing. This hunt focuses on the narrow point between replay and lasting access. It looks for a session whose MFA requirement was met by a token claim, that shows up from a second ASN, and that then writes to the directory and the mailbox.

## Why

- Huntress linked this campaign (July 14 to September 22, 2026) to 79 compromised organizations and at least 48 rogue Entra devices, with replay as soon as four minutes after capture. The rogue device and swapped MFA methods survive a password reset, so the window between replay and lasting access is the cheapest point to remove the attacker.
- Replay infrastructure changed during the campaign (1337 Services GmbH hosting, then ExpressVPN or CrownCloud), but the behavior did not. A replayed token has to carry the victim's original MFA claim and session identity across a network boundary, and lasting access requires directory and mailbox writes that are audited and can be tied back to that same session.
- Every leg shows up in standard Microsoft telemetry: interactive and non-interactive sign-ins, Entra audit Device and Authentication Methods activity, and Exchange Online audit operations. Session ID and IP/ASN joins connect them without endpoint coverage, which matters because the victim's phishing device is often unmanaged.
- False positives drop sharply once a persistence write is required. Carrier handoffs and travel rarely pair an ASN hop with a self-initiated device registration and MFA method replacement within 24h. Helpdesk resets carry an admin initiator, and new-device setup follows fresh interactive MFA from the user's usual network.
